For educators

Show how things work.
Let students build them.

Textbooks show machines standing still. GearScript shows them moving, from a pendulum to a gearbox, and lets students change one number and watch what happens. It is free, it runs in any browser, and there are no accounts for students to create.

A short explainer, in an afternoon

Flipped lessons need short videos, and video editing takes time teachers do not have. In GearScript the story is written next to the machine:

  • Narration spoken by a natural voice, from lines you type
  • Captions burned into the video or saved as .srt and .vtt, for accessibility and for students learning in a second language
  • Chapters joined into one video with fades
  • Sounds that follow the motion: a tick on every swing, a knock when a part lands
  • Frames for slides, YouTube, Shorts and Reels
// a pendulum that ticks, with a caption
Narrator::Voice { captions: on; }
Tick::Sound { kind: tick; }

@motion
Scene {
  0s..60s: sound: Tick every 1s;
  1s..5s:  say: Narrator "One swing, one second.";
}

Put it in your course

Export the animation as a single .html 3D player. Students can play, pause, scrub and turn the model themselves, and it needs no install. Upload the file to your course site, or embed it in Moodle, Canvas, Google Sites or any page that accepts an iframe:

<iframe src="pendulum.html" width="800" height="500" style="border:0" allowfullscreen></iframe>

MP4, WebM and GIF work everywhere else: slides, learning platforms and messaging apps.

As an assignment, not just a demonstration

They build it, so they understand it

Ask students to make a mechanism move a certain way. Writing it shows whether they understand it, not whether they can draw it.

Errors that teach

More than 100 numbered problems, each with a plain reason and a fix: E0353: The linkage locks at 1.20s, then what to change. It is a patient tutor at 11 at night.

Easy to collect and check

Projects are plain text, so they are easy to submit, compare and keep. The command line can check a folder of submissions and report where parts are at any moment.

gearscript check  submissions/aisha.gscript --json        # does it compile, and what is wrong
gearscript sample submissions/aisha.gscript --at 0,1,2   # where each part is at 0, 1 and 2 seconds

Private by design

No accounts, no sign-in and nothing uploaded: students’ work stays on their own device unless they save or share it. That makes GearScript simple to use in schools. See the privacy policy.

With an AI assistant

Prompt AI copies a short guide to the language for any AI assistant. Teachers can draft a lesson animation from a sentence; students can ask an AI for help and still have to understand what it wrote.

Ready in ten minutes

  1. Open the studio and take the short tour.
  2. Open an example close to your topic, from the Examples menu or the gallery.
  3. Change one number, such as a length or a speed, and watch the whole mechanism respond.
  4. Add a line of narration and a caption.
  5. Export a video for your slides, or a 3D player for your course page.
