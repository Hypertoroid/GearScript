Why GearScript

Animate how a machine works,
not how it looks.

Most animation tools move pictures. GearScript moves mechanisms. You write what the parts are and how they connect, turn one of them, and the rest follows the way real parts would. No keyframes, nothing to install, and the source is a short text file that you, or an AI, can read and change.

Three ideas behind it

Correct because it is connected

Hinges, sliders, pins, gears, belts and rolling contact are part of the language. A solver closes every loop at every frame, so a linkage moves the way it really would, or the studio tells you exactly where it locks.

Text you own

A project is a short, CSS-like file. It works with any editor, version control and code review, it never gets locked in a format, and changing a length or a speed is one edit, not an evening of re-keying.

Written and fixed by AI

The syntax is small and predictable. Every problem has a number, a reason and a fix, and an AI can reply with a patch that changes only what needs changing. The studio checks the patch before anything is applied.

When not to use GearScript

Honesty saves you time. Reach for another tool when you need:

  • Forces, stress or physics. GearScript solves how parts move (kinematics). It does not compute loads, friction, deformation or dynamics, so it does not replace FEA or a physics simulator.
  • Photorealistic renders or character animation. Its looks are clear and attractive, but it is not a film renderer, and it has no skeletal rigs for people or creatures.
  • Manufacturing drawings. Use CAD for tolerances, drawings and parts you will make.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. GearScript is open source under the Apache License 2.0. You can use it for any purpose, including commercial work, and run your own copy.

Who owns what I make?

You do. You own the code you write and the animations you export, and we claim no rights in them. You are responsible for the rights to anything you add, such as models, pictures, audio and text. See the terms of use.

Do I need to install anything or make an account?

No. The studio runs in your browser. There are no accounts, and you never sign in. You can also install it as an app from the studio’s More menu.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After your first visit the studio is kept in your browser, so it opens and exports without a connection. A neural voice for narration is downloaded the first time you use one.

Is my work uploaded anywhere?

No. Your projects and files stay on your device unless you save or share them yourself. The privacy policy explains the little the site does collect.

Can an AI write GearScript for me?

Yes. In the studio, Prompt AI copies a short syntax guide for any AI assistant. Paste its reply back, and the studio checks it and explains any problem with a fix. Developers can use the command line and MCP server; see GearScript for AI.

What can I export?

MP4 and WebM video with sound and captions, GIF, PNG frames (with transparency), captions as .srt or .vtt, a self-contained .html 3D player anyone can turn and scrub, and the project itself as text.

How hard is it to learn?

If you have seen CSS, you can read it at once. The studio has a tour, hints as you type, dozens of examples and full documentation. Most people have something moving within minutes.

Is it finished?

It is a public beta: version 1.0 beta. The language may still change before 1.0; from 1.0 on, projects that work keep working. See what’s new.
